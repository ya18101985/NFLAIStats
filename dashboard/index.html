    How the win chance is computed

    Each leg starts from a pregame expectation for the player (yards as a normal distribution, catches and touchdowns as Poisson counts). As the clock runs, only the remaining share of the game is projected, nudged up to 40% toward the player's in-game pace, and added to what is already on the board.

    Spread and total legs project the remaining points from each game's market total and spread. Legs on the game score (spreads, totals, first-half lines) are simulated together from the live score, since a big lead makes a cover and an over more likely at the same time. Player legs are multiplied in as if independent. Legs on the same player move together in reality, so stacked same-player combos are a little better than shown when he is having a big game and a little worse when he isn't.

    A player marked out gets no further production. Live data comes from ESPN's game feed and refreshes throughout game day until every game is final.
